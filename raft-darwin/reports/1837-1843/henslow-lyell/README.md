# Henslow and Lyell: Luna first reading, parent adjudication

The 1837–1843 Henslow/Lyell review adds **two confirmed incoming → Darwin reply pairs**: **425 → 428** and **701 → 705**. It also adds six knowledge/reuse attestations and four replies written by correspondents. The latter preserve conversation structure but never supply Darwin's voice or prove his receipt.

Separate `gpt-5.6-luna` reviewers read Henslow and Lyell first. A third Luna checked dates and authorship; follow-up readings checked specialist context and the competing antecedents for 712. The primary agent then read the retained bodies, headers and notes, checked the competing sources, and adjudicated both accepted and unpaired cases. Original proposals remain separate from the accepted map.

All **56 safe correspondence bodies** were read in full. Four further incoming sources—534, 690, 706 and 708—were read with their Darwin witnesses. 690 retains its original 1809 lower XML bound and its period hold. Demonstrated knowledge does not silently resolve composition eligibility. Babington 708 says he **saw** comparable plants; Darwin 712 says Babington **reared** a facsimile. The attribution/content match is supported, but that paraphrase is not independent proof of an experiment.

**712 remains without a uniquely identified surviving prompt.** 701 contains both Polygonum names and the soil warning; 707 contains later Atriplex clarification but not that disclosure. Darwin's 710F already relays the Polygonum information before 712. An earlier letter can receive another response, and an unlocated follow-up is also possible. Neither the latest surviving date nor the phrase “last note” settles which document is meant. The first Luna's proposed 707 → 712 pair is therefore held. The independent check's stronger assertion of a definitely separate missing note is also not adopted.

604 → 602 remains a candidate reverse reply: 602 expects to return on July 15/16 and promises another note if plans change, while 604 answers a decision to stay. An intervening update may be the actual prompt. Similarly, 425 → 480 is held because shell-less deposits do not uniquely identify the separate decaying-shell information Darwin says was supplied.

## Date and attribution decisions

- **356:** the written date may be Henslow's receipt annotation. Original XML May 28, 1837 is preserved; unconditional composition dating is held.
- **376 and 425:** preserve their two dated incoming sections, without turning composition into receipt.
- **649:** October **5 and 7**, not alternative dates. “Friday morning” falls inside extracted paragraph 4. Character offsets split that block exactly, preventing the later section from inheriting the earlier date. The retrospective Council report does not establish a Friday meeting.
- **428 and 432:** preserve manuscript inconsistencies and the editor's resolved calendar corrections; brackets or corrections alone do not require a date hold.
- **421F:** a petition to Thomas Spring Rice, signed by Darwin, Lyell and others. Signatures do not establish Darwin's individual authorship. It is outside the Darwin–Lyell exchange and excluded from voice.
- **699F:** an online placeholder and editorial summary, not surviving Darwin prose. Excluded from full-body, grounding and response counts.
- **505:** retain only Henslow's note to Darwin; the underlying Herbert-to-Henslow letter is third-party material.
- **724:** preserve diagram/table structure; its labels are not ordinary prose to be flattened at export.

The ledger records 24 reference observations, distinguishing letters, proofs, parcels/documents, uncertain routes, Darwin's own writing and unresolved incoming identity. These are **not a count of distinct missing physical letters**. Later publication or citation of incoming 579, 582 and 598 does not prove knowledge before 1844.
