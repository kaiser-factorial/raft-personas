# Receipt evidence around letters 301–306

This is a limited manual supplement to the XML date audit. Three incoming letters can be connected to Darwin's acknowledgments; it is not a receipt audit of all 126 incoming letters. The original metadata and period classifications remain intact.

## What the letters establish

| Evidence | Finding | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [301, 29 April 1836](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/letter/?docId=letters/DCP-LETT-301.xml), Mauritius | Darwin describes the date of his latest received letter as thirteen months earlier, while discussing news from home. | Distinguish the age of the letter from the date it arrived. Do not extrapolate a precise exclusion date for every correspondent. |
| [302, 3 June 1836](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/letter/?docId=letters/DCP-LETT-302.xml), Cape of Good Hope | He acknowledges Catherine's January letter and says older correspondence remains missing. | A newer letter can become available while earlier letters remain unavailable. |
| [304, 9 July 1836](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/letter/?docId=letters/DCP-LETT-304.xml), St Helena | He describes the missing mail to Henslow and supposes it includes a letter from him. | The disruption concerns more than family mail, but the particular Henslow letter is unidentified. |
| [305, 18 July 1836](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/letter/?docId=letters/DCP-LETT-305.xml), approaching Ascension | A final, undated flap note reports an unopened bundle as the outgoing letter must leave. | The existence of a mail bundle does not establish that its contents were read before this response. |
| [306, 4 August 1836](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/letter/?docId=letters/DCP-LETT-306.xml), Bahia | He acknowledges receiving Catherine's October and Susan's November letters at Ascension, after the newer Cape letter. | These two older letters are available by this response; their exact delivery days remain unresolved. |

Darwin's expectation in 302 that the missing mail would not reach him until after England is a prediction. Letter 306 supplies later evidence that some of it reached him sooner. Preserve the prediction as his belief at the time without turning it into an actual delivery date.

The 296→302 identification is supported by sender and month plus the printed extracts, Erasmus's household, and the expected Parker child. The 288→306 identification also has a content match: Susan passes on Sedgwick's praise, to which Darwin responds. The 287→306 identification rests on the named sender and month within this sequence. These are high-confidence identifications of surviving records, not letter numbers explicitly supplied by Darwin.

## Application to the persona corpus

Keep the original composition date alongside receipt evidence and its source. Use 296 as established inbound context for 302, and 287 plus 288 as established inbound context for 306. These links need not exhaust everything influencing either reply.

For general memory retrieval, a conservative policy activates 296 on 3 June and 287/288 on 4 August, the dates by which receipt is attested. These are deliberately conservative availability dates, not asserted delivery dates. Same-day ordering requires additional evidence; the named responses already provide that evidence. Raft conversion has not been performed.

Letter 301 supplies a constraint on home-news availability, not a blanket rule removing every incoming letter written in the previous year. Check each incoming letter or identifiable batch. Outgoing letters with no established inbound link remain dated grounding.
